§ 14denoise — the plain loop

Thirty-nine lines, and the one that runs for every FLUX.2 checkpoint except the KV variant with references loaded. It is also the only one of the three where the references are re-processed from scratch at every step.

14.1 · What the model actually sees

Because no num_ref_tokens is passed, the references are not "conditioning" in any structural sense — they are extra image tokens. They attend to the canvas, the canvas attends to them, the text attends to both, and all of them are modulated by the same timestep vector. A reference photograph, which is a perfectly clean image, is processed as though it were as noisy as the half-finished canvas it is guiding.

14.2 · Three details worth knowing

The slice is unconditional in practice. Line 302 tests if img_input_ids is not None — but img_input_ids is img_ids, which the caller always provides, so the test always passes. Without references the slice is a no-op (the prediction is already exactly img.shape[1] wide); with them it discards 15 876 columns of computed predictions. The guard reads as if it were protecting against a missing-ids case that the signature does not allow.

The concatenation is rebuilt every step. torch.cat on lines 292–293 allocates a fresh 16 900-token tensor at each of the N iterations, copying the references every time. Since neither the reference tokens nor their ids change, that copy is pure overhead — small next to the transformer call, but not zero: at 512² with one reference it is 4.3 MB per step in bfloat16.

The model returns more than the sampler wants. Flux2.forward strips the text tokens but not the reference tokens, so the sampler has to. The cached path is the opposite: forward_kv_extract strips both before its final layer, which is why denoise_cached contains no slicing at all.

▸ Why the ordinary forward pass builds a cache it throws away

In model.py, Flux2.forward runs its blocks through forward_kv_extract with num_ref_tokens=0 and assigns the returned cache to _. One code path serves both modes: with zero reference tokens the "extract" is a no-op that costs a few tensor slices, and the model does not need a second implementation of its own forward. The sampler never sees any of this — it just calls model(...).

§ 15denoise_cached — the two-phase loop

Forty-six lines that look almost exactly like denoise and behave completely differently. The references enter the network once, at a timestep of zero, and every later step reads their keys and values out of a dictionary.

15.1 · What is in the dictionary

The sampler treats kv_cache as an opaque object — it never indexes it, never inspects it, never copies it. Everything it contains is decided by model.py:

KeyContents
double_blocksOne entry per double-stream block: the keys and values the reference tokens produced in that block, for both of its two streams.
single_blocksOne entry per single-stream block: the same, for the merged sequence.
num_ref_tokensHow many tokens those entries describe — the number the cached forward needs in order to splice them back into the attention.

For klein-9B that is 32 entries of K and V over 15 876 tokens, which is the 524 288 bytes per token of §7.2 — 8.59 GB for four references at 1024². The sampler's contribution to that cost is exactly zero lines of code: it holds the dictionary in a local variable and hands it back.

⚠ The two loops are not interchangeable

It is tempting to read denoise_cached as "the same thing, but faster". It is not the same thing. In denoise the references are modulated at the current timestep and attend to the canvas as it evolves; in denoise_cached they are modulated once at t = 0 and their keys and values never change. The two produce different numbers, which is why the repository ships a separate checkpoint, flux.2-klein-9b-kv, for this path — and why the script chooses between them with model_info.get("use_kv_cache") and ref_tokens is not None rather than offering a flag.

✦ Why step 0 is allowed to be expensive inference

The first step of the cached loop is more work than a step of the plain loop: it processes the references, the canvas and the text, and it stores K and V for every block on the way. The saving only starts at step 1. With four steps that is one expensive step and three cheap ones — which is why the published speedups are modest at four steps (1.78× with one reference) and why the trick is worth much more at fifty. The measured numbers, from the repository's own benchmark document, are 1.78× and 2.66× at 512², and 1.21× and 1.85× at 1440², where the canvas is big enough that the references matter less.

§ 16denoise_cfg — the doubled loop

Forty-seven lines for the checkpoints that were not distilled: no guidance input, so guidance is performed from the outside, by running the unconditional and the conditional prompt through the network in one doubled batch and extrapolating between the two answers.

16.1 · Why the halves stay in step

After the combine, both halves of img hold the same numbers: line 406 duplicates the guided prediction, and line 408 adds it to both. From that point the two halves are identical, and they stay identical for the rest of the generation — the only thing that differs between them is the text they are paired with, which the network sees but the image does not.

So the second half is redundant state. It costs a tensor copy per step and a doubled batch in the next call, which is not redundant at all: that call needs both halves, because guidance is a difference between two predictions and neither one can be computed without running its prompt. The alternative — keeping one image and duplicating it inside the loop — would allocate the same doubled tensor every step instead of once. inference

▸ The order of the halves is a contract, and it is written in a comment

pred_uncond, pred_cond = pred.chunk(2) assumes the unconditional prediction is in row 0. Nothing in this function enforces that; what enforces it is the caller, which builds ctx = torch.cat([ctx_empty, ctx_prompt], dim=0) — empty first. Swap the two there and every generation would be extrapolated in the wrong direction, away from the prompt, with no error and no exception. The comment on line 368 exists to stop exactly that.
