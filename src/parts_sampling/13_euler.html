§ 3One line of arithmetic: the Euler step

Fifty network calls produce fifty predictions. The image is what you get by adding them up — and "adding them up" is a single line, repeated verbatim in all three loops of this file. It is the only place where the schedule of §2 touches a pixel.

img = img + (t_prev − t_curr) · pred (t_prev − t_curr) < 0, always

Three facts make this line readable. First, pred is the network's estimate of the velocity — the direction from the clean image toward the noise, v ≈ x₁ − x₀, which is what a rectified-flow model is trained to output. Second, the ladder of §2 descends, so t_prev − t_curr is negative and the product points against the velocity: toward the image. Third, the size of that negative number is the stride, and it is the whole contribution of the schedule.

◆ Why one step would be enough on a straight path

Suppose the path really is the straight line x(t) = (1−t)·x₀ + t·x₁, so the velocity is the constant v = x₁ − x₀. Start at noise, t = 1, and take one step with t_prev − t_curr = 0 − 1 = −1:

x1 + (−1)·(x1 − x0) = x1 − x1 + x0 = x0

Exactly the image, in one call. Every extra step exists for one reason only: the learned velocity field is not perfectly straight and not perfectly predicted, so the sampler re-asks along the way and corrects. This is also why the strides of §2 must sum to −1 — the loop has to travel the whole path, no more. VERIFIED: for the 4-, 8-, 28- and 50-step ladders the sum of t_prev − t_curr is −1.0000000 in every case.

3.1 · The same line, three times

There is no solver abstraction, no step function, no scheduler object. Each of the three loops ends with its own copy of the update, written out in full:

LoopLineWhat surrounds it
denoise305 img = img + (t_prev - t_curr) * pred — after one call that may have carried reference tokens, whose predictions were just thrown away (§14).
denoise_cached353 The same line, reached either from forward_kv_extract (step 0) or forward_kv_cached (every step after) — the arithmetic does not care which (§15).
denoise_cfg408 The same line, but pred has already been combined from two halves of a doubled batch (§16).

That triplication is a deliberate trade: the three loops differ in what they feed the model and what they do with the answer, and each is short enough to read top to bottom in thirty seconds. There is no shared base class to go and look at.

3.2 · The time the model is told, and the time the model receives

One detail of this line's neighbourhood matters more than it looks. The stride t_prev − t_curr is computed from Python floats and keeps full precision. But the time handed to the network is cast first:

t_vec = torch.full((B,), t_curr, dtype=img.dtype) and img.dtype is bfloat16

bfloat16 carries an 8-bit significand, so between 0.5 and 1.0 the representable times are spaced 2−8 = 0.00390625 apart. Near t = 1 the schedule's strides are much finer than that — at 2048×2048 with 50 steps the first stride is 0.0008 — so several consecutive rungs of the ladder land on the same bfloat16 number.

3.3 · What the step does not do

Not hereConsequence
No clipping or renormalising The latent is never projected back onto a plausible range. Nothing stops an over-guided run from drifting; the guard is the guidance value itself (§6).
No added noise The trajectory is deterministic — an ODE solve, not an SDE. The only randomness in a generation is the initial randn.
No solver memory Previous predictions are never reused (no Heun, no DPM-Solver multistep). Each step is a fresh first-order estimate, which is what makes the KV-cache path of §15 possible: nothing about the solver state has to be kept consistent between two different call shapes.
No partial-image tricks No inpainting mask, no latent blending, no img2img start at t < 1 — although starting the ladder lower would be all it took, since the loop only ever reads timesteps.
