§16Flux2 — three ways to run the network · L52

The Flux2 class is mostly bookkeeping: it owns the embedders, the modulation MLPs, the two block stacks and the output head, and then offers three forward passes that differ only in which tokens they carry and what they remember. Understanding the difference between them is understanding the whole inference design.

The third forward is a mirror of the second with three differences: no x_seq_concat argument, no reference modulation, and the cache supplied instead of built.

⚠ The two cached paths are not interchangeable

sampling.py can drive references two ways, and they are not the same computation. denoise concatenates the references onto the canvas and calls forward(), which hard-codes num_ref_tokens=0 (L150, L162): nothing is isolated, the references attend to the noisy canvas, they are conditioned at the current timestep, and they are recomputed on every step. denoise_cached calls forward_kv_extract once and forward_kv_cached thereafter, where the references are sealed off (§13.2) and conditioned at t = 0.0. Different mathematics ⇒ different images, which is why the cached variant ships as its own checkpoint (flux.2-klein-9b-kv) rather than as a flag on the normal one.

Token order is not what distinguishes them. Positions travel with their tokens through RoPE, so [img, ref] and [ref, img] describe the same computation up to a permutation of rows. What matters is whether the references are isolated and frozen.
