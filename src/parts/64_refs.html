§19References & further reading

  • The source. black-forest-labs/flux2, src/flux2/model.py — the file this article walks through, line by line. Also sampling.py, autoencoder.py, text_encoder.py and docs/flux2_klein_kv_cache.md in the same repository.
  • FLUX.2 technical write-up. Black Forest Labs, "FLUX.2" blog post (bfl.ai/blog/flux-2) and the accompanying representation-comparison note — the closest thing to a paper; there is no arXiv preprint for FLUX.2 itself.
  • Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers (SD3 / MM-DiT). Esser et al., 2024 — arXiv:2403.03206. Where the double-stream block comes from — two weight sets meeting in one joint attention — together with rectified-flow training, the timestep-shift formula of §1.3 and logit-normal timestep sampling during training. What does not come from SD3: the hybrid double-then-single stack (SD3 is double-stream throughout) and the single shared modulation (SD3 modulates per block, as DiT does).
  • RoPE. Su et al., "RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding", arXiv:2104.09864 — the rotation mechanism generalised to four axes in §4.
  • SwiGLU. Shazeer, "GLU Variants Improve Transformer", arXiv:2002.05202 — the silu(x₁)·x₂ gate of §9.2.
  • Classifier-free guidance. Ho & Salimans, "Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance", 2022 — arXiv:2207.12598. The two-pass extrapolation of §5.3 that use_guidance_embed lets a distilled model skip.
  • adaLN-single. Chen et al., "PixArt-α: Fast Training of Diffusion Transformer for Photorealistic Text-to-Image Synthesis", 2023 — arXiv:2310.00426. The observation that per-block modulation is a large share of a DiT's parameters, and the shared-vector answer to it; §5.7 compares the two against what FLUX.2 does.
  • FiLM. Perez et al., "FiLM: Visual Reasoning with a General Conditioning Layer", 2018 — arXiv:1709.07871. Shift-and-scale conditioning in its general form; adaLN is FiLM applied at a layer norm.
  • Sinusoidal encodings. Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need", 2017 — arXiv:1706.03762. Where the 128-frequency fingerprint of §9.1 comes from, originally as a position encoding.
  • adaLN. Peebles & Xie, "Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers" (DiT), arXiv:2212.09748 — adaptive layer norm with shift/scale/gate from a condition vector.
  • Demystifying Flux Architecture. arXiv:2507.09595, July 2025 — an independent review of FLUX.1, written before FLUX.2 existed. Read it for FLUX.1's design (and for the hybrid double/single recipe FLUX.2 inherits); it says nothing about this file. The FLUX.1 → FLUX.2 deltas quoted in §7 (19+38 → 8+48 blocks, Mistral-Small-3.2 replacing T5-XXL + CLIP) come from BFL's own release material, not from this paper.
  • QK-norm. Henry et al., "Query-Key Normalization for Transformers", 2020 — the stability trick behind §9.7.

All numerical claims in this article — parameter counts, token counts, FLOPs, cache sizes, mask patterns, rotation orthogonality and the bit-exactness of the KV cache — were computed or measured directly from the repository's code, not copied from documentation. Statements about why the authors made a choice are a different kind of claim: the file carries almost no comments, so those are marked inference wherever they appear.
