§15DoubleStreamBlock · L524

The first eight blocks of the network are different. Text and image keep separate weights — separate norms, separate projections, separate MLPs — and meet only inside the attention, where their queries and keys are concatenated so each modality can read the other. This is the MM-DiT idea from Stable Diffusion 3, and it is what lets a word-vector and a pixel-vector, which live in completely different statistical worlds, share one attention operation without one corrupting the other.

◇ Why only eight of these?

Double-stream blocks are where cross-modal understanding is built; single-stream blocks are where it is refined. The hybrid recipe itself is FLUX.1's, not SD3's — SD3's MM-DiT is double-stream all the way down — and no public ablation says where the optimum lies. What the file does show is the direction of travel: FLUX.1 used 19 double + 38 single, FLUX.2 uses 8 + 48, spending its extra depth on the cheap side of the boundary. inference A double block costs exactly 2× a single one, so at equal parameter budget more single blocks means more layers.
