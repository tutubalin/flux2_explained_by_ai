§ 2FLUX.2's structure — three models, one job

Before any of it can be explained, one thing has to be said clearly, because it decides what model.py is and is not: FLUX.2 is not one model. It is three, bolted together, and the file this article walks through is only the middle one. The other two exist for a single reason — the middle one cannot read a word, and it cannot see a pixel.

2.1 · What the transformer never sees

The middle box has no idea what a word is and no idea what a pixel is. Its inputs are, in full:

inputshape, at 1024²where it came from
the canvas4 096 rows × 128 numberspure Gaussian noise, reshaped — no model produced it
the prompt512 rows × 15 360 numbersthe text encoder (§3)
reference imagesup to 16 384 rows × 128the VAE encoder (§4), if this run has any
positionsone row of 4 integers per tokena counter, no model at all (§7)
the timeone floatthe schedule (§1)
the guidanceone floata setting you chose (§8)

Everything else — the meaning of the prompt, the look of a photograph, the fact that the output is supposed to be a picture of a cat — has already been converted into arithmetic by the time this file sees it. That is worth internalising before reading a single line of code, because it explains two things that otherwise look like odd choices. The network needs no vocabulary and no image handling, so it has none. And its output is not an image either: it is 4 096 rows of 128 numbers describing which way to move, which the sampler adds to the canvas and the VAE eventually turns into pixels.

2.2 · What runs how often

One asymmetry explains where all the effort in this file goes.

modelcalls per imageits sizewhat that implies
text encoder124 B (dev), 8 B / 4 B (klein)huge but amortised: its cost does not grow with the step count, so a bigger, better language model is affordable
VAE encoder0–10small next to the other twoonce per reference image, before the loop starts
VAE decoder1once, after the last step
transformer4 – 5032.2 B (dev)every step, on every token — so this is where speed, memory and precision are all spent, and why 56 blocks of it are engineered down to the last bias term

Read the last row against the first: the text encoder is three quarters the size of the transformer and runs once. The transformer runs fifty times. Almost every decision in model.py — the shared modulation, the parameter-free norms, the fused projections, the KV cache for reference tokens, the absence of a single bias — is a decision about the cost of one call, multiplied by the number of steps.

💡 Why a 24-billion-parameter language model is a reasonable text encoder

Because it runs once. Its cost is a fixed entry fee, not a per-step tax, so FLUX.2 can afford a text encoder larger than most image generators are in total — and gets, in exchange, an understanding of prompts that a small CLIP-style encoder cannot match: long sentences, negations, counts, and legible spelling in the image itself. §5 is about that model and the strange shape of its output. inference — the sizes and the single call are in the code; the reasoning about why that trade is worth making is not written down anywhere.
