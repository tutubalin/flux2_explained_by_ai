§ 5One language — how 128 and 15 360 both become 6 144

The transformer has exactly one internal width. Whatever arrives — a patch of a photograph, a reference image, a piece of your sentence — has to be re-expressed as a row of 6 144 numbers before it can take part, and whatever leaves is 6 144 numbers too. Two matrices do the translating. They are the first thing Flux2.forward touches and among the simplest objects in the file, but nothing downstream makes sense until you have seen why they exist and what they cost.

5.1 · Two doors, one corridor

doorwhat walks inwhat it isparametersand what walks out
img_inthe canvas, and any reference imagesone matrix, 128 → 6 144, no bias786 4324 096 rows of 6 144 at 1024² — plus up to 16 384 more if references are supplied
txt_inthe prompt, as the language model left itone matrix, 15 360 → 6 144, no bias94 371 840512 rows of 6 144

Both are a single nn.Linear with bias=False — no nonlinearity, no second layer, no normalisation. One matrix multiply per token, and that is the whole door.

5.2 · Widening and narrowing are the same operation

The two doors look opposite — one multiplies the width by 48, the other divides it by 2.5 — but they are the same kind of thing: a change of basis. Each output number is a learned weighted sum of the inputs, and nothing else happens.

doorwhat the arithmetic doeswhat it cannot do, and why that is fine
128 → 6 144
widening		spreads each token's 128 latent numbers across 6 144 coordinates, in the directions the network's weights expectIt creates no information: 6 144 numbers that live in a 128-dimensional subspace still carry 128 numbers' worth. That is enough — the point is not more content but a shared format, so that the same 56 blocks can act on a patch and on a word with the same matrices.
15 360 → 6 144
narrowing		selects and combines: the language model's three-depth description is far richer than the corridor, so the matrix learns which of it to keepIt cannot keep everything — 15 360 dimensions cannot fit into 6 144 without loss. What is dropped is whatever 56 blocks of image-making turned out not to need, and that was settled during training, not by anyone reasoning about it.
💡 Why the doors are plain matrices, while the time door is an MLP

Compare them with the two doors that are still to come — the ones for the timestep and for the guidance value. Those are time_in and guidance_in, and each is two linear layers with a SiLU between them: 256 → 6 144 → 6 144, 39 321 600 parameters apiece. Why the difference? Because of what arrives. A prompt row has already been through a 24-billion-parameter language model and a patch row through a trained autoencoder — their meaning is computed, and all that is left is a change of basis, which one matrix does. A timestep arrives as a 256-number code produced by a fixed formula (§7 builds that code, §9 feeds it in) — numbers with no learned meaning in them yet. Something has to decide what "t = 0.62" means, and that something is the second layer. inference — the shapes are in the file; the reasoning about why one door needs depth and the other does not is not written down anywhere.

5.3 · Why 6 144, of all numbers

The corridor's width is not free. It has to divide evenly into attention heads, because a head is just a slice of the row: head_dim = hidden_size // num_heads. FLUX.2 keeps the slice size fixed at 128 across all three checkpoints and varies the number of slices instead:

checkpointhidden_sizeheadshead_dimimg_intxt_in
FLUX.2 [dev]6 14448128786 43294 371 840
FLUX.2 [klein-9B]4 09632128524 28850 331 648
FLUX.2 [klein-4B]3 07224128393 21623 592 960

A fixed head width means the part of the architecture that is most geometry-sensitive stays constant while the model is scaled: every head, in every checkpoint, is 128 numbers = 64 rotation planes, so the RoPE ladder of §8 has the same shape everywhere and the attention temperature 1/√128 never changes. Shrinking the model removes heads, not resolution within a head.

⚠ Two different 128s

They are unrelated and it is very easy to conflate them. in_channels = 128 is how many numbers one token carries into the network: 4 latent cells × 32 channels (§4). head_dim = 128 is how wide one attention head is inside the network: hidden_size ÷ num_heads. One describes the outside world, the other the corridor. They would still both be 128 if the other were 96.

5.4 · The exit door

After 56 blocks the rows are still 6 144 wide, and they are still not an image — they are the network's opinion about which way each token should move. Turning that back into latents takes one more projection, and it is the mirror of img_in: 6 144 → 128, again a single bias-free matrix.

The exit has one thing the entrance does not: its own modulation. final_layer normalises each row with a parameter-free LayerNorm, applies a shift and a scale derived from the same condition vector vec that steered all 56 blocks — a fourth, private set of dials — and only then projects down. There is no gate, because there is no residual stream left to add anything to: this is the last operation, so "contribute nothing" is not an option it needs.

What comes out is 4 096 rows of 128 numbers, which is folded back into a 128 × 128 × 32 latent grid and handed to the VAE decoder — the only component in the whole system that has ever seen a pixel (§4.2).

◇ The shape of a whole run, in six lines

noise (B, 4096, 128) → img_in → (B, 4096, 6144)
prompt (B, 512, 15360) → txt_in → (B, 512, 6144)
concatenate → (B, 4608, 6144) → 8 double blocks (two streams) → merge → 48 single blocks
drop the text rows → (B, 4096, 6144) → final_layer → (B, 4096, 128)
reshape → (B, 32, 128, 128) latents → VAE decoder → (B, 3, 1024, 1024) pixels
Every one of those arrows is either a matrix multiply or a reshape. Repeat the middle of that fifty times and you have generated an image.
