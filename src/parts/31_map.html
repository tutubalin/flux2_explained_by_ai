§ 6The file at a glance

Before diving in, here is the entire file on one screen. Fourteen classes and seven module-level functions, listed in the order they appear. Click any row to jump to its explanation.

The file starts with seven lines of imports, and they are the whole of its dependency list — no local modules, no configuration files, nothing hidden:

math is used exactly once (the log in timestep_embedding, §9.1). dataclass and field build the three configuration objects (§8). torch, nn and F supply the layers and scaled_dot_product_attention (§10). Tensor is imported as a name so the type hints read x: Tensor. And rearrange, from einops, does every reshape in the file — including the one that splits 6144 features into 48 heads of 128 (§11).

◇ Reading order

The file is written top-down — the giant Flux2 class comes first, at line 52, and the tiny helpers it depends on come later, near line 800. That is the opposite of how you should read it. This article is deliberately the other way round, in five parts: Part 1 teaches the ideas with no code at all, Part 2 maps the file (you are here), Part 3 walks the eleven small parts in dependency order, Part 4 assembles them into the blocks and the model, and Part 5 zooms back out to the loop, the design decisions and a cheat sheet. By the time you reach Flux2.forward there will be nothing left in it that you have not already met.
