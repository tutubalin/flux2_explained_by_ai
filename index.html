§ 1What FLUX.2 actually is

You ask for "a red fox in snow at dusk". FLUX.2 does not draw the fox. It starts from pure static and, over 50 small corrections, pushes that static until it becomes a fox. model.py is the thing that decides which way to push.

FLUX.2, released by Black Forest Labs in November 2025, is a latent flow-matching image model. Three separate networks cooperate:

  1. A vision-language model (Mistral-Small-3.2, 24B parameters) that reads your prompt and produces a list of meaning-vectors. This is the part with world knowledge — it knows what a fox looks like, what "dusk" implies about lighting, and how to spell words you ask it to render.
  2. A variational autoencoder (VAE) that shrinks images by 16× in each spatial direction. Instead of pushing around a million pixels, the model pushes around four thousand compact latent tokens. This is what makes the whole thing computationally possible.
  3. The rectified-flow transformer — this file — which repeatedly looks at the half-finished image plus the meaning-vectors and answers one question: "what should change, and by how much?"

The count above is 32,223,281,152 — that is why FLUX.2 is advertised as "32B". My closed-form formula reproduces it perfectly for all three configs:

A double block costs exactly 2× a single block

981,467,648 versus 490,733,824. Not a coincidence: a double block carries two complete sets of attention and MLP weights (one for text, one for image), while a single block carries one set shared by everything. This is why FLUX.2 can afford only 8 double blocks but 48 single ones.

The whole model has no bias terms at all

I enumerated every parameter: 0 of the 32.2 billion are biases. All 171 linear layers use bias=False, and all 81 LayerNorms use elementwise_affine=False. The only non-matrix parameters are 128 RMSNorm scale vectors of length 128 — 16,384 values in total, about 0.00005 % of the model.

Modulation is global, not per-block

In the original DiT and in FLUX.1, every block owned its own adaLN modulation matrix. FLUX.2 hoists them out: there are exactly three Modulation modules in the entire network, computed once per step and handed to all 56 blocks. Had each block kept its own, the modulation alone would have cost roughly 11 billion extra parameters.

There is no dropout anywhere

Zero nn.Dropout modules. At 32B parameters trained on a very large dataset, regularisation by dropout is unnecessary — the model is far from overfitting. Inference and training graphs are therefore identical, which also makes the code simpler to read.

§ 2Rectified flow in five minutes

Everything in model.py follows from one equation. Understand this section and the rest of the file is bookkeeping.

2.1 · The single idea

Take a real image and call it x₀. Take pure Gaussian noise and call it x₁. Now draw a straight line between them and put a dot somewhere on that line. The dot's position is described by one number, t:

xt = (1 − t)·x0 + t·x1 with t = 0 → clean image,   t = 1 → pure noise

That is the entire "forward process". There is no gradual noise schedule, no Markov chain, no β-parameters — just a line segment. The model's whole job is to learn the velocity: the direction and speed you would have to travel along that line. Formally it learns the vector field

v(xt, t) = dxtdt = x1 − x0

Notice what that means: the target the network is trained to predict is simply noise minus image. Not the noise, not the clean image — the difference. This is why FLUX.2 is called a flow model rather than a diffusion model: it is trained to integrate an ordinary differential equation along (approximately) straight paths, following Lipman et al. 2023 and Liu et al. 2023.

2.2 · Reading the sampling step

With that picture in mind, the denoising loop in sampling.py is three lines:

Line 212 is exactly the pink arrow in Figure 2. t_curr is where we are, t_prev is where we want to be next (always a smaller number), pred is the velocity v that Flux2.forward just returned. Multiplying velocity by the time difference gives the displacement. Nothing else happens — no noise is injected, no variance schedule is applied. It is a plain, deterministic Euler integration of an ODE.

⚠ Gotcha · which direction is "forward"?

The convention here is t = 1 is noise and t = 0 is data — the opposite of what many diffusion tutorials use, where t counts up as noise is added. In this codebase get_schedule returns timesteps starting at 1.0 and ending at 0.0, and the Euler step moves down. Keep that in mind whenever you see a timestep value: big = noisy, small = clean.

2.3 · The timestep schedule is not uniform

If you split [1, 0] into 50 equal slices you waste steps in the easy middle of the trajectory and starve the hard part. Which part is hard? Empirically, the noisy end: deciding the global composition from near-pure noise is where most of the information is created, and it is also where the velocity field curves the most. So FLUX.2 warps the uniform grid through a shift function, taken from the Stable Diffusion 3 paper:

t′ = eμeμ + (1/t − 1)σ μ = f(number of tokens, number of steps), σ = 1

§ 3From pixels to tokens: what the model actually eats

model.py has no convolutions and no image logic. It receives images that have already been flattened into a list of vectors. To understand its shapes you must know exactly how that flattening happens.

3.1 · Three stages of compression

The VAE in autoencoder.py does two of the three stages; the third happens in sampling.py before the call:

◇ Beginner · why bother with latents at all?

Attention costs grow with the square of the token count. A 1024×1024 image as raw pixels would be over a million tokens: 10¹² attention pairs per layer, utterly impossible. As 4096 tokens it is 21 million pairs — expensive but feasible on real hardware. The latent space is a compression scheme that throws away perceptually irrelevant detail (exact sub-pixel noise) while keeping everything a human would notice. FLUX.2's VAE was retrained specifically to make that trade better at high resolution than FLUX.1's.

3.3 · Text tokens, and the mystery of 15360

The text side is simpler but has one surprise. The 24-billion-parameter Mistral VLM is run over your prompt, and then three of its internal layers — layers 10, 20 and 30 — are pulled out and stacked side by side:

And the reason the Klein configs use 12288 and 7680 is exactly the same trick with different teachers: Qwen3-8B (4096 × 3) and Qwen3-4B (2560 × 3). Using mid-depth layers rather than only the last one gives the flow transformer access to both syntactic and semantic features — a trick also used in SD3.

Prompts are padded and truncated to MAX_LENGTH = 512 tokens, which is where the "512 text tokens" column above comes from.

3.4 · Reference images are just more tokens

For editing and multi-reference generation, reference images go through the same VAE encoder and the same patchify, and their tokens are simply appended to the sequence. Up to ten references are supported; to keep the cost sane the pipeline caps reference pixels at 1024² each when there are several, and 2024² when there is only one. The only thing that distinguishes a reference token from a canvas token is its position id — which is the subject of the next section.

§ 4Position: the four-axis rotary embedding

Attention, by itself, is blind to order. Feed it the same tokens shuffled and it returns the same answer shuffled. Everything spatial about an image — and everything sequential about a sentence — has to be injected deliberately. FLUX.2 does it with RoPE, extended to four axes at once.

4.1 · The one-paragraph version of RoPE

Rotary Position Embedding (Su et al., 2021) works by rotating the query and key vectors in 2-dimensional planes before the dot product. Suppose a head has 2 dimensions and a token sits at position p. We rotate its query by an angle p·ω:

q(p) → [ cos pωsin pω −sin pωcos pω ] · q and the magic: ⟨Rp q, Rp′ k⟩ depends only on p − p′

Rotations preserve lengths and, crucially, composing two rotations subtracts their angles. So the attention score between two tokens automatically encodes their distance, with no extra parameters and no fixed maximum sequence length. That relative-distance property is what makes RoPE generalise to image sizes never seen in training — essential for a model that must handle anything from 512² to 4 MP.

◆ Verified · the property holds in this exact implementation

I rotated a fixed query/key pair with apply_rope at several position pairs and measured the dot product. Pairs with the same gap give the same score, to six decimals:

gap = 2 : (p=0, p=2) → 4.919082   (p=5, p=7) → 4.919081   (p=13, p=15) → 4.919082
gap = 3 : (p=0, p=3) → 6.703997   (p=5, p=8) → 6.703996

I also confirmed each produced matrix is a genuine rotation: RᵀR = I to 6×10⁻⁸ and det R = +1.000000, and that apply_rope leaves every vector's norm unchanged.

4.2 · FLUX.2's twist: four positions per token

A language model needs one position number per token. FLUX.2 needs four, because its tokens live in a 3-D volume of images stacked over a "time" axis, plus a separate index for text. Every token therefore arrives with a coordinate vector ids of shape (B, L, 4):

💡 Why references sit at t = 10, 20, 30 …

The canvas and the text both live at t = 0. Each reference image is pushed ten units further along the t axis. Because RoPE makes attention scores depend on coordinate differences, a reference at t = 20 is "far away" from the canvas at t = 0 in exactly the same mathematical sense that token 20 is far from token 0 in a sentence. The model therefore knows, from geometry alone, that these tokens are a different image, not a distant part of the canvas — and it can still reach them, because attention is global. ◇ Beginner · why is theta only 2000?

Language models use θ = 10 000 or more because their positions run into the thousands and they need very slow frequencies to distinguish position 4000 from 4001. FLUX.2's coordinates are tiny by comparison — grid indices under ~200, text indices under 512, and a t axis that only ever reaches about 100. A smaller base keeps the slowest plane from being uselessly slow over such short ranges, so the whole frequency ladder stays inside the range of distances the model actually needs to resolve.

§ 6The eleven small parts

Every large structure in this file is assembled from eleven small ones. We now meet each of them, line by line, in the order that makes them easiest to understand — not the order the file happens to list them in.

6.1 · timestep_embedding(t, dim, max_period, time_factor) — L710

The model needs to know how noisy its input is. But a single float like 0.87 is a terrible input for a neural network: one number cannot be meaningfully multiplied by a 6144-column weight matrix in a way that distinguishes 0.87 from 0.86. The standard fix, inherited from the original diffusion paper and from Transformers' positional encoding, is to expand the scalar into a bundle of sines and cosines at many different frequencies.

6.5 · RMSNorm and QKNorm — L734, L746

Attention scores are dot products of queries and keys. During training those vectors can grow large, and a few oversized keys can dominate every softmax in the layer — a well-known instability. The fix used here, from Henry et al. / Zhang & Sennrich and popularised for flow transformers by SD3, is to normalise queries and keys per head before the dot product.

§ 7causal_attn_fn — the attention router · L758

Fifty-eight lines that decide, for every query in the sequence, which keys it is allowed to read. Despite its name there is nothing autoregressive here — the "causality" is of a different, more interesting kind.

⚠ Gotcha · the name lies

In an LLM, "causal attention" means token i may not see tokens after it — the triangular mask that makes text generation possible. causal_attn_fn contains no triangular mask at all: all three of its scaled_dot_product_attention calls pass is_causal=False. What it implements instead is a block-diagonal isolation rule: reference tokens are causally cut off from the rest of the sequence — they may read each other, and nobody's updates may leak into them. That single property is what makes the KV cache of §12 mathematically exact. Read the name as "references are sealed", not as "left-to-right".

§ 9DoubleStreamBlock · L524

The first eight blocks of the network are different. Text and image keep separate weights — separate norms, separate projections, separate MLPs — and meet only inside the attention, where their queries and keys are concatenated so each modality can read the other. This is the MM-DiT idea from Stable Diffusion 3, and it is what lets a word-vector and a pixel-vector, which live in completely different statistical worlds, share one attention operation without one corrupting the other.

◇ Why only eight of these?

Double-stream blocks are where cross-modal understanding is built; single-stream blocks are where it is refined. Empirically (and this is the central scaling result of the SD3 paper) a few joint-weight blocks followed by many shared-weight blocks buys most of the quality at a fraction of the cost. FLUX.1 used 19 double + 38 single; FLUX.2 moved even further along that trade, to 8 + 48 — spending its extra parameters on the cheap side of the boundary.

💡 Why blend instead of two passes?

You could run the reference tokens through every block separately with their own modulation, then run the image tokens — but then the image tokens could not attend to the references, which is the entire purpose of having them. Blending the modulation per token lets one pass carry two different conditioning regimes through the same attention.

§ 12The KV cache: why it is exact, and what it costs

Everything in §6–§11 was building towards this. The claim: reference tokens produce byte-identical keys and values at every denoising step, so they can be computed once and replayed. Nothing in model.py states this as a theorem — it emerges from three design decisions that only make sense together.

Running forward_kv_extract at timestep 0.9 and again at 0.1 produced reference keys and values that were bit-identical — not merely close. Feeding the 0.9 cache into forward_kv_cached at timestep 0.5 and comparing against forward_kv_extract at 0.5 gave a maximum absolute difference of 0.000e+00 across all outputs. The optimisation is exact.

The cache is not free: it stores two tensors per block per reference token, in the network's working precision. The formula is

§ 13The denoising loop — where model.py meets the world

model.py never loops. It answers one question per call: "given this noisy image at this timestep, which way is clean?" The loop lives in sampling.py, and it is short enough to fit in your head.

Read the update rule slowly, because it is the whole of rectified flow: the model predicts a velocity — the direction and speed at which the image should move — and the sampler takes one Euler step of size (t_prev − t_curr) along it. At the last step t_prev = 0, landing exactly on the clean latent, which the autoencoder then decodes into pixels. There is no noise injected anywhere: this is an ordinary differential equation being integrated, not a stochastic process.

§ 14Why the file looks like this — twelve design decisions

Reading 834 lines you meet a lot of choices that look arbitrary until you see the reason. Here they are, collected.

decisionwherethe reason
Fused projectionsL453, L459One matrix produces q, k, v and the MLP input; another consumes attention and MLP output together. Five matmuls become two, and on a GPU fewer, bigger matmuls are dramatically more efficient.
